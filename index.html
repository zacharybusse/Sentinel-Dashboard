How the $12.65M forecast is built

Actual cost ($7.8M) + estimate to complete ($4.85M) = $12.65M bottom-up EAC. The CPI-based cross-check gives ~$12.78M; Finance recommends the bottom-up figure as the base planning forecast.
What the $12.65M does not include: a dollar figure for unresolved risk (EMV), and any assumption of a battery hardware redesign. Layering the battery-risk EMV brings the risk-adjusted forecast to $12.77M — a $270K gap against the $12.5M authorization, still inside the $335K of remaining reserves. The cost-plus contract structure allows a contingency review if the gap needs to close further.

          Decision & Path Forward

          The question in front of the board is not whether SENTINEL is viable — Phase 2 answered that. It is whether to commit the final phase of funding now, and on what terms.

          Decision Options

          NO-GO (Risk-Averse Path)

          • Hold Phase 3. Require 85% integration coverage before proceeding.
          • Slip to Nov/Dec. Integration work would extend into December.
          • Consequences: 30–45 day slip; impacts device production; customer timeline at risk.
          • Upside: higher test coverage; more risk retired.

          GO with Conditions — Recommended

          • Authorize Phase 3. Accept 72% coverage + conditions.
          • Hit Dec 31. Phase 3 closes as planned with a 71-day buffer.
          • Risk mitigation: monthly governance review, Sep 15 integration gate, Sep 28 battery decision.
          • Upside: deploy on schedule; manage risk actively.

          Why GO with Conditions

          • Technology is proven: the MVP works. Pilot feedback is 90% positive (Logistics) and actionable (Field Ops). No unknown unknowns remain.
          • Schedule is protected: a 71-day buffer runs from the Oct 21 Phase 2 close to the Dec 31 deployment, at risk only if the battery decision slips beyond Sep 28.
          • Money is tight, but covered: $12.65M EAC vs. $12.5M authorization is a $150K base gap ($270K risk-adjusted), within the remaining $185K contingency and manageable under the cost-plus contract. Condition #6 keeps it visible.
          • Conditions are management, not engineering: all six are management and governance actions — none require new technology or architectural change.
          • Timing is right: starting Phase 3 now, rather than waiting on Sep 28, allows parallel resolution while procurement and build prep proceed. Waiting costs three weeks of buffer.
          • Sep 28 is a hard gate: the battery decision is binary. If Path A fails, the board escalates to Path C with pre-authorization, retaining full authority to redirect.
          Assessment: risk is elevated but not unmanageable. Proceeding with conditions, paired with the Sep 28 battery gate and monthly governance reporting, gives the board visibility and decision authority throughout Phase 3.

          Sep 28 Decision Gate · Slide 06

          Battery Challenge: Three Modeled Paths

          Software Optimization

          Cost: $50K | +0 weeks

          Adaptive sync, GPS duty-cycling, low-power fallback. ~4 weeks engineering, parallel with pilot. 48–60 hr battery life. Marcus Chen (Software Lead) leading; Jennifer Morales (Product Owner) approves; delivers by Sep 25.

          Hybrid

          Cost: $150K | +2–3 weeks

          Minor hardware change plus software optimization. 2 wks HW + 4 wks integration. 72 hr+ — meets target.

          Hardware Redesign

          Cost: $400K | +4–6 weeks

          Full battery/power-subsystem redesign. Draws management reserve; likely a funding conversation. 96 hr+ — exceeds target.

          All three paths still hit Dec 31 inside the 71-day buffer. Sep 28 is a cost-and-quality choice, not a go/no-go.
