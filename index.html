How the $12.65M forecast is built

Actual cost ($7.8M) + estimate to complete ($4.85M) = $12.65M bottom-up EAC. The CPI-based cross-check gives ~$12.78M; Finance recommends the bottom-up figure as the base planning forecast.
What the $12.65M does not include: a dollar figure for unresolved risk (EMV), and any assumption of a battery hardware redesign. Layering the ~$240K of validated risk EMV brings the risk-adjusted forecast to roughly $12.89M — near the edge of the $335K of remaining reserves.

          Decision & Path Forward

          The question in front of the board is not whether SENTINEL is viable — Phase 2 answered that. It is whether to commit the final phase of funding now, and on what terms.

          Decision Options

          NO-GO / Redirect

          Hold Phase 3 pending further technical clarity; extend Phase 2 into Oct/Nov. Risk: consumes the buffer and puts Dec 31 deployment in question.

          Conditional GO — Recommended

          Authorize Phase 3 now. Six conditions resolve in parallel, all by Sep 15/25. The Sep 28 battery decision is a hard gate; the board may escalate if Path A fails.

          GO / Unconditional

          Authorize Phase 3 with no conditions. Not recommended: risks Phase 3 entry with known testing gaps and an unresolved battery path.

          Why GO with Conditions

          • Technology is proven: the MVP works. Pilot feedback is 90% positive (Logistics) and actionable (Field Ops). No unknown unknowns remain.
          • Schedule is protected: a 71-day buffer runs from the Oct 21 Phase 2 close to the Dec 31 deployment, at risk only if the battery decision slips beyond Sep 28.
          • Money is tight, but covered: $12.65M EAC vs. $12.5M authorization is a $150K gap, within the remaining $185K contingency. Condition #6 keeps it visible.
          • Conditions are management, not engineering: all six are management and governance actions — none require new technology or architectural change.
          • Timing is right: starting Phase 3 now, rather than waiting on Sep 28, allows parallel resolution while procurement and build prep proceed. Waiting costs three weeks of buffer.
          • Sep 28 is a hard gate: the battery decision is binary. If Path A fails, the board escalates to Path C with pre-authorization, retaining full authority to redirect.
          Assessment: risk is elevated but not unmanageable. Proceeding with conditions, paired with the Sep 28 battery gate and monthly governance reporting, gives the board visibility and decision authority throughout Phase 3.

          What the Board Approves

          1. Authorize a Phase 3 baseline of $3.7M — $1.5M hardware, $1.8M software, $0.4M integration.
          2. Accept the $185K remaining contingency as sufficient, and pre-authorize the ~$130K of anticipated draws with monthly tracking (Condition #6).
          3. Preserve the $150K management reserve intact; confirm the drawdown rule — Sponsor under $50K, Board at or above $50K.
          4. Ratify the six governance conditions, escalating Condition #1 for immediate action (Marcus Chen) — Condition #3 (PO allocation) is already resolved.
          5. Confirm Sep 28 as a firm decision gate, and Oct 5 and Dec 15 as the remaining Phase 3 gates.
          6. Delegate integrated HW/SW change-control authority to the weekly Leadership Team for changes under $25K; board review at or above.

          Critical Sep 28 Decision Gate · Slide 06

          Battery Challenge — 3 Paths

          Issue: Prototype #2 battery performance meets spec at 70°C lab, but field validation data shows degradation under sustained 45°C ambient. Sep 28 decision required to lock Phase 3 approach.

          Path A: Software Optimization

          Cost: $50K | Feasibility: 70%

          Aggressive power-down and battery monitoring SW. Marcus Chen (Software Lead) leading; delivers by Sep 25.

          Path B: Hybrid (Optimize + Component)

          Cost: $150K | Feasibility: 10%

          SW optimization + alternative battery component. High risk; limited vendor availability.

          Path C: Hardware Redesign

          Cost: $400K | Feasibility: 20%

          Full battery/power subsystem redesign. Schedule impact: 60–90 days, consumes contingency.

          Board Decision: Recommend Path A (70% feasibility, $50K, Sep 25 result drives Sep 28 gate). Board should pre-authorize Path C as fallback if Path A fails.
